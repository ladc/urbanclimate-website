We are proud to announce that our paper on the LEGO Urban Climate Workshop has been published in the scientific journal *Urban Climate*! The publication was announced on *Dag van de Wetenschap* (Science Day), where we once again welcomed young city builders to our workshop at the Wetenschapsfestival in Brussels.

### From LEGO city to temperature map

The urban heat island effect is well established in the scientific literature, but the physics behind it can feel abstract to non-experts. Our workshop, designed as a serious game, makes it tangible. Participants build a miniature city out of LEGO bricks, which is scanned and translated into a land-cover map. An AI model, trained on data from the VLINDER citizen science measurement network, then instantly calculates the temperature pattern across the city on a warm, calm summer night. Swap a parking lot for a park, and you see the effect right away.

![Close-up of a LEGO city built during the workshop](images/events/photos/wetenschapsfestival2026_1.jpg)

### What did participants learn?

Between 2023 and 2025, we held the workshop at public science events in Brussels, mainly for children aged 7 to 12. Across four editions in 2024 and 2025, 400 participants filled in a short questionnaire after the workshop. The results are encouraging:

- **86.2%** identified built-up areas as the warmest parts of the city;
- **92.5%** identified green areas as the coolest;
- **90.5%** chose a greener urban design as the better option for warm nights;
- **77.1%** said they had learned something new.

The answers were consistent across the different events. This shows how a data-driven urban temperature model can turn a complex climate concept into an engaging, hands-on experience.
